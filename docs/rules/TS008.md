# TS008 nesting-depth

The severity is warning; the rule is on by default and stable.

## Reports

The control flow nests at most four levels inside a function, so it stays readable without a map.

Reporting unit: Each block that opens a level past the limit. An `else if` chain reports once
rather than once per arm.

Anchor: The block's opening keyword.

Carve-outs: An `else` or `else if` arm continues its chain rather than opening a level, and a
`switch` or `match` arm, a `case` clause included, is a case rather than a level. A Python `with`
and `async with` count as a level, as do an `except`, a `catch`, and a `finally`, each at the depth
of the `try` it belongs to; the body block a `with` or a `try` owns is part of that construct and
adds nothing of its own. A block whose first statement is a `with` is not that `with`, so a second
`with` beside the first, or an `if` after it, sits at the first one's depth rather than one deeper,
and a `with` that opens a `try` body counts beside the `try`. The count runs inside a function and
resets at each function, and in Zig a `comptime` block and a `test` block are scopes of their own. A
labelled block is not a scope of its own: `blk: {` is nesting like any other block, and the branches
inside it count from the function, not from the label.

A construct with no braces counts too, since the language allows `if (a) break;` and its
depth is one past the block that holds it. A header that spells two constructs counts two
levels, as `while (true) switch (x) {` does. A construct that stands as a value is not a
level unless it carries a block: that is how a Python ternary, a JavaScript ternary, a
comprehension clause, a Rust match guard, and a braceless Zig `if` expression on the right of
`=` stay out of the count.

## Does not report

The rule reaches every language tigerstyle-lsp lints.

A finding is suppressed by a `tigerstyle-ignore:` directive naming TS008, by a
`tigerstyle-file-ignore:` directive, or by a `tigerstyle-disable:` region covering the line.

## Known false positives

The shapes that misfire here are `zig-else-clause` and `nested-statement-count`.
